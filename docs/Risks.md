# Risks

Risks that could stop pix.tacocat.com moving from AWS to Cloudflare, and what's been done to mitigate. Timings are [Globalping](https://globalping.io) probes from Paris, Marseille, Baton Rouge and Los Angeles, and `api/scripts/probe.sh` from Berkeley, with the D1 primary in SJC and read replication on. "Worker" is time spent inside the Worker; TTFB adds the visitor's connection. How performance is judged against the AWS site, and every measurement behind it, is in `docs/Perf.md`.

Status: ✅ solved, ⚠️ solved with a catch, ❌ fails today, ⏳ open or untested.

| 1   | TTFB > 100 ms                                        | ❌     | When a read reaches an active replica, TTFB is great: 41 to 130 ms (17 to 70 ms in the Worker), but the first read from a colo does not: after 4 and 8 idle hours on 2026-09-23, the first album read from Paris and Baton Rouge went to the SJC primary every time, TTFB 444 to 667 ms from Paris (338 to 631 ms in the Worker) and 225 to 408 ms from Baton Rouge (67 to 301 ms in the Worker), while California stayed at 17 to 43 ms in the Worker. Six rounds on 2026-09-26, 22 idle minutes apart, put that first read from Paris at 355 to 652 ms in the Worker whether the read sends D1 one statement or two, whether the primary is idle or awake, and whether the album has 4 rows or 33; the colo's next read of the primary took 180 to 237 ms, about one round trip; and a Frankfurt or Marseille replica answered from the second or third read on, in 21 to 103 ms, then went inactive again within 22 minutes. Woken from San Jose the primary answered in 70 ms. So what a far reader pays is the colo's first contact with the D1 instance, not the query, the isolate or an idle primary (`docs/Perf.md`, 2026-09-26). |

The AWS image CDN's misses take 271 ms at the median and 388 ms at p90. It is one step of a page load; what it costs the page is judged in `docs/Perf.md`. Album JSON is not edge-cached for the same reason: in a week of the AWS site's logs, most album views were the first of that page at that edge, a 35 to 40% hit rate (`docs/plans/EdgeCachedAlbums.md` in `tacocat-gallery-sam`). Next: a second derived bucket in WEUR, whose cold read from Paris is unmeasured.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        |

| 13  | R2 event notification to Queue to Worker is not a usable upload pipeline     | ✅     | Tested from a browser on staging on 2026-09-25: 52 JPEGs and HEICs, then 21 PNGs twice, all became items with thumbnails, HEIC decoding and orientation included, through CORS, the R2 event notification, the queue and the real Images binding. | 16  | Upload metadata extraction differs from Lambda                               | ✅     | Solved 2026-09-25. Workers have no `DOMParser`, and ExifReader skips the XMP packet without one, where in Node it loads `@xmldom/xmldom` by itself; the Worker now passes that parser in. It mattered more here than on AWS, which converted every HEIC to JPEG before reading it: a HEIC or PNG carries no IPTC, so a caption written in Adobe Bridge is XMP only, and every such upload had lost its title and keywords. `readImage` now takes the same fields in the same order as the Lambda's `selectMetadata`: IPTC Object Name, Headline, then XMP title and headline for the title; IPTC Caption/Abstract then XMP description; IPTC Keywords then XMP subject for the tags, which the Worker had not read at all, and IPTC Object Name under its real name, which it had misspelt. The unit test is the Lambda's own fixtures with the values its tests expect, so parity is what the test asserts; the HEIC cases fail without the parser. Reading a 497 KB HEIC or a Bridge-captioned JPEG takes about 1 ms in workerd, 4 ms at worst, on a clock 1 ms coarse. The bundle grew from 1371 to 1602 KiB, 267 to 315 KiB gzipped. GIF is untested. Fields Bridge writes that the Lambda ignored, city, country and copyright, are ignored here too.                                                                                                                                      |
| 23  | Images binding leaves the original's metadata in JPEG derivatives            | ⚠️     | Tested against the real binding on 2026-09-26 with `/2025/09-29/a_hotel_imperial1.jpg`, a 2.9 MB iPhone JPEG carrying 58 KB of metadata: its 200x200 JPEG thumbnail came out 49,284 bytes, of which 36,530 were the original's Photoshop IPTC block (21,446) and XMP packet (13,902) byte for byte, plus 1,166 of EXIF with camera, dates and GPS position, over about 12,750 of pixels. WebP came out 14,588 bytes and AVIF 11,606, carrying nothing, as documented. Post-fix AWS makes the same thumbnail at about 12,500 bytes, after hitting the same wall in Sharp and writing no EXIF at all (`tacocat-gallery-sam` 53a0ab9); its earlier derivatives, `/2025/09-29` among them, still carry a 21 KB embedded preview until regenerated. Thumbnails are now WebP, with JPEG only for a browser whose `Accept` lacks `image/webp`; the media page's image stays JPEG, with the metadata, since readers drag it into apps that cannot open a WebP. Untested: whether the zone's URL transformations (`/cdn-cgi/image/`) honour `metadata=none`, since they are not enabled.                       |

## Admin

| #   | Risk                                           | Status | Result                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
| --- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 17  | Passkey-only admin login is too much machinery | ✅     | Works, on an iPhone too: `/login`, `/invite/<token>` and a signed 30-day session cookie. A passkey is bound to the hostname, so an `Origin` allowlist picks the site, and `wrangler dev` needs `--local-upstream`. A login challenge lets someone in once: a D1 row records it until its cookie expires, since synced passkeys report a sign count of 0 and the count cannot catch a replay. A session cannot be revoked early: removing an admin means deleting their passkeys and rotating `SESSION_SECRET`, which logs every admin out. Who may be invited is the `user` table, seeded by a migration rather than a screen, and the passkey and invite tables reference it. |

| 19  | Three environments and CI cannot be driven from config          | ⚠️     | `infra/` (OpenTofu) declares the zone, bot and cache settings, D1, the buckets, the R2 custom domain, the queue and its notification; `api/wrangler.jsonc` owns the Worker, bindings, routes and crons, and the D1 schema ships as migrations. Provider gaps: no per-category AI crawler policies; R2 custom domains and event notifications cannot be imported; importing D1 needs `ignore_changes` on the location hint. One account API token has to be made by hand, since Wrangler's OAuth login stops at `zone:read`. Staging and production are two Workers from one `api/wrangler.jsonc`, each with its own data from an OpenTofu module instance (Environments in `README.md`). Deploys run in GitHub Actions (`.github/workflows/deploy.yml`) against GitHub Environments that `scripts/github-setup.sh` creates, with one account API token as the repository secret, the one thing made by hand; Workers Builds was tried first and dropped, since its deploys showed nowhere in GitHub and every setting of it was a dashboard field. The release is `scripts/release.sh` (Deploying in `README.md`): a version uploaded without traffic, the migrations, a check at 0% through the version-override header on the live hostname (a Worker with a Container gets no Version URL), then the switch, and a rollback if the live check fails. A pull request branch releases to staging on every push and a merge to `main` releases production, so merge is the promote. Not built: per-branch previews (Worker Previews, shipped 2026-09-22, do not reach the queue or the crons) and an alert on production errors after a release. |
